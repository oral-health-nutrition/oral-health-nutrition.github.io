Building a national evidence base on diet, nutrition and oral health across the life course

Based at Dublin Dental University Hospital and Trinity College Dublin, our programme spans six interconnected research strands, from infant nutrition through to cognitive health in ageing.

Dublin Dental University Hospital Trinity College Dublin HRB • DAFM • DDUH funded

Research programme

Six interconnected strands

Strand 1

Education and Diet Assessment Methodology

A new nutrition curriculum at DDUH trains 180+ dental students per year in dietary assessment using digital tools, from peer diet workshops to OSCE-assessed case portfolios. This work contributed to an international competency framework for diet and nutrition in oral health education.

DDUH funded
Strand 2

Oral Health in Irish Elite Athletes

In collaboration with the Sport Ireland Institute, we collected the first national data on diet and oral health in Irish elite athletes. Despite high nutritional awareness, 90% of athletes surveyed had tooth decay. Ongoing work includes pre-competition screening and an oral health guide for sports staff.

Sport Ireland Institute
Strand 3

Oral Health and Infection Risk in Intestinal Failure

A cross-sectional study at the St James's Hospital national intestinal failure referral centre found untreated dental caries in 72% of patients and unexpectedly high oral MRSA colonisation (17%), raising the question of whether the oral cavity is an unrecognised reservoir for catheter-related bloodstream infections.

DDUH funded
Strand 4

SWEETWISE: Reducing Sugar in Infant Foods

A DAFM-funded consortium across TCD, UCD and TU Dublin addressing the high free sugar content of commercially available complementary foods for infants in Ireland. TCD/DDUH's role is developing a novel in vitro cariogenicity model to test whether reformulated infant foods reduce cariogenic potential.

DAFM funded — €1.3M consortium
Strand 5

HRB Fluoride Programme: Oral Health and Cognition

Ireland's largest oral health research strand, examining community water fluoridation, oral health and cognitive outcomes using data from TILDA and Growing Up in Ireland. Our analysis of individual-level geocoded lifetime fluoride exposure found no association with cognitive function in older adults (IRR = 1.00, fully adjusted).

Health Research Board — SDAP-2025-017
Visit the fluoride programme site →
Strand 6

Oral Health Trajectories in Children and Young People

Using longitudinal data from the Growing Up in Ireland study, this strand examines how oral health behaviours and outcomes develop from early childhood through adolescence. Applying life-course epidemiological methods across GUI Cohorts '98, '08 and '24, the research identifies critical periods for prevention and the drivers of oral health inequality.

Trinity Research Doctorate Award 2026
